Habits that well-known founders describe or recommend

23 people, 47 mentions we could open, checked on 26 Oct 2026. What they say, what research we opened, and what to take from it.

What this page is, and what it is not

  • A sample, not a poll. The counts are of different people in the 23 we chose and could source, not of all founders. 44 mentions are counted (14 recommend, 30 describe); 3 are counter-views, shown separately and not counted.
  • Survivorship. We hear the routines of people whose firms succeeded. Failed founders with the same habits are invisible.
  • Reverse causation. Money buys sleep, gyms, help and time, so habits may follow success rather than cause it.
  • Media selection. Magazines run routine stories, and tidy or extreme routines are over-represented. Many routines here are reported by a journalist, not the founder; each entry says which.
  • Promotion. Some sources sell something: a book, a podcast with sponsors, a wellness company. We name it where we saw it.
  • Not medical advice. Nothing here tells you to change your sleep, food or exercise. See a doctor for that.

Habit by habit

For each habit: who says it and in what words (quotes are short and checked against the page), what research we opened, if any, and a small experiment that is Our suggestion, not the founders’ claim.

Our suggestion How to try any of these. Try one change for two to four weeks, note one simple measure (for example tasks finished, or mood out of five), then keep it or drop it. Change one thing at a time. This is not medical advice.

Move your body most days (often early)

8 people: 2 recommend it, 6 describe their own practice only. Theme: Body.

Recommend it to others

  • Richard Branson tells others to: “you don't have the time unless you make the time” (Richard Branson’s blog on virgin.com, 23 Jun 2019; own words.)
  • Nithin Kamath tells others to: “A workout in the morning also increases the odds of making healthier choices” (Nithin Kamath’s blog, 30 Dec 2022; own words.)

Describe their own practice

  • Sam Altman describes doing it: “Exercise is probably the second most important physical factor.” (Sam Altman’s blog, undated page; earliest archive copy 10 Apr 2018; own words.)
  • Tim Cook (CEO of Apple, not a founder) describes doing it: “I go to the gym and work out for an hour” (CNBC, 27 Nov 2018; as reported.)
  • Jack Dorsey describes doing it: “Dorsey says he has walked the five miles from his home to his office” (CNBC, 8 Apr 2019; as reported.)
  • Sridhar Vembu describes doing it: “a long walk and, on occasion, a swim in the village well” (Forbes India, 3 Jun 2020; as reported.)
  • Anand Mahindra (chairman of a family-founded group, not a founder) describes doing it: “I rotate my weekly fitness routine between cardio-vascular” (Indian Express, 23 Jun 2025 (article); post from 2022; as reported.)
  • Nikhil Kamath describes doing it: “at night, when I work out” (Indian Express, 28 Mar 2025; as reported.)

What research we opened

  • World Health Organization, physical activity fact sheet (June 2024). At least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week is the recommendation, and 31% of adults worldwide do not reach recommended levels. The page links activity to lower risk of heart disease, cancer and diabetes and fewer symptoms of depression and anxiety. It says nothing about morning versus evening.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. A 20-30 minute walk or workout at a fixed time, 3 days a week, to start.

Protect your sleep

5 people: 2 recommend it, 3 describe their own practice only. Theme: Body.

Recommend it to others

  • Arianna Huffington tells others to: “Instead of bragging about our sleep deficits” (TED talk transcript, Dec 2010; own words. The wording is TED's description of her talk, not a transcript. She later founded Thrive Global, which sells sleep and wellness products.)
  • Nithin Kamath tells others to: “Sleep has to be, by far, the most underrated aspect of good health.” (Nithin Kamath’s blog, 30 Dec 2022; own words.)

Describe their own practice

  • Sam Altman describes doing it: “Sleep seems to be the most important physical factor in productivity for me.” (Sam Altman’s blog, undated page; earliest archive copy 10 Apr 2018; own words.)
  • Jeff Bezos describes doing it: “I get eight hours of sleep. I prioritize it” (Economic Club of Washington transcript, 13 Sep 2018; own words.)
  • Elon Musk describes doing it: “I tried sleeping less, but then total productivity decreases” (CNBC, 12 Feb 2021; as reported. CNBC quotes a podcast; we did not open the podcast.)

Counter-view (not counted)

  • Nikhil Kamath describes doing it: “I struggle with five or five and a half hours.” (Indian Express, 28 Mar 2025; as reported. A counter-view: he does not recommend short sleep.)
  • Radhika Gupta (asset-manager CEO, not a founder) describes doing it: “I am usually short of sleep because I travel and work late nights” (LinkedIn post, about March 2024 (page shows only "2y"; inferred from Women's Day tag); own words. The date is our estimate: the post shows only “2y”.)

What research we opened

  • American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research Society consensus statement, 2015. Adults should sleep seven or more hours a night to promote health; under seven is linked with weight gain, diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, depression and higher risk of death.
  • CDC, About Sleep (page dated 15 May 2024). Its table gives 7 or more hours for ages 18 to 60, 7 to 9 for 61 to 64, and 7 to 8 for 65 and over.

Nikhil Kamath says he gets five to five and a half hours and Radhika Gupta says she is usually short of sleep; both are below the seven or more the guidance gives. The guidance is for adults in general; individual need varies.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. Pick a fixed bedtime and phone-off time; keep it for 2 weeks.

Read a lot

5 people: 2 recommend it, 3 describe their own practice only. Theme: Mind.

Recommend it to others

  • Naval Ravikant tells others to: “Read what you love until you love to read” (Naval Ravikant’s blog, 3 Apr 2019; own words.)
  • Bill Gates tells others to: “Reading fuels a sense of curiosity about the world.” (TIME, 22 May 2017; own words. The TIME page carries unrelated sponsored-content text above the interview, and Gates Notes also promotes book lists.)

Describe their own practice

  • Warren Buffett (investor, not a founder) describes doing it: “I probably read five to six hours a day.” (Farnam Street, date and interviewer not stated on page; as reported. Low confidence: the page gives no date or interviewer. Undated; we did not find the original interview.)
  • Sridhar Vembu describes doing it: “I do a lot of reading, entirely non-fiction.” (The Hindu BusinessLine, 7 Feb 2013; as reported.)
  • Nikhil Kamath describes doing it: “while I read a book every morning” (Indian Express, 28 Mar 2025; as reported.)

Evidence not checked We opened no study on this habit. The founders’ own words above are all we have.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. 15 minutes of a book you enjoy daily (Naval's "read what you love").

Sit quietly / meditate

5 people: 2 recommend it, 3 describe their own practice only. Theme: Mind.

Recommend it to others

  • Naval Ravikant tells others to: “I recommend meditating one hour each morning” (The Almanack of Naval Ravikant, page shows "Aug 12", no year; own words. Compiled by a third party; undated. The Almanack is a commercial book, free to read online.)
  • Ray Dalio (founded an investment firm; not a typical founder) tells others to: “Meditation is ‘the single most important reason’ for my success” (CNBC, 16 Mar 2018; as reported. The headline wording is CNBC's; we did not open his own post. He sells books.)

Describe their own practice

  • Jack Dorsey describes doing it: “I've more or less kept up the practice of two hours” (CNBC, 8 Apr 2019; as reported.)
  • Tim Ferriss (author and podcaster, not a company founder) describes doing it: “Number two, I meditate” (The Tim Ferriss Show transcript (PDF), 18 Sep 2015; own words. From the show's own transcript. He sells books and has podcast sponsors.)
  • Anand Mahindra (chairman of a family-founded group, not a founder) describes doing it: “the most important part of a daily health routine is 20 minutes of meditation” (Indian Express, 23 Jun 2025 (article); post from 2022; as reported.)

What research we opened

  • Goyal and colleagues, JAMA Internal Medicine, 2014 (review of 47 trials, 3,515 people). Mindfulness programmes gave moderate evidence of less anxiety, depression and pain, and low evidence for stress and quality of life. There was no evidence that meditation beat other active treatments such as exercise.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. 5-10 minutes of quiet sitting before opening your phone.

Describe their own practice

  • Tim Cook (CEO of Apple, not a founder) describes doing it: “Tim Cook wakes up at 4 a.m.” (CNBC, 27 Nov 2018; as reported.)
  • Richard Branson describes doing it: “Richard Branson wakes up at 5:00 am everyday” (CNBC, 10 Nov 2016; as reported.)
  • Nithin Kamath describes doing it: “Sleeping by 9 pm & getting a workout at 5 am has been helpful.” (Nithin Kamath’s blog, 30 Dec 2022; own words.)
  • Sridhar Vembu describes doing it: “A typical day begins at 4 am” (Forbes India, 3 Jun 2020; as reported.)
  • N. R. Narayana Murthy describes doing it: “He still wakes up at 5 in the morning” (Indian Express, 25 Sep 2011; as reported.)

Counter-view (not counted)

  • Radhika Gupta (asset-manager CEO, not a founder) describes doing it: “wake up at 5 am, have a warm glass of water, meditate” (LinkedIn post, about March 2024 (page shows only "2y"); own words. She is describing what influencers do. She does not follow it.)

Evidence not checked We opened no study on this habit. The founders’ own words above are all we have.

We opened no research linking an early start to success. Several people here are wealthy enough to choose their hours, so the habit may follow success, not cause it.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. Do NOT copy a 4-5 am wake time by itself; no evidence opened that early rising causes success.

Guard focused time; cut or time-box meetings

4 people: 1 recommend it, 3 describe their own practice only. Theme: Time and focus.

Recommend it to others

  • Elon Musk tells others to: “it is rude to make someone stay and waste their time” (CNBC, 18 Apr 2018; as reported. An internal email as reported; we did not open the email. It was an instruction to his staff.)

Describe their own practice

  • Sam Altman describes doing it: “I try to do meetings in the afternoon.” (Sam Altman’s blog, undated page; earliest archive copy 10 Apr 2018; own words.)

  • Paul Graham describes doing it: “we can meet with them, and lose half a day's work” (Paul Graham’s essay, Jul 2009; own words.)

Evidence not checked We opened no study on this habit. The founders’ own words above are all we have.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. Block your best 2 hours with no calls; push meetings to the afternoon.

Describe their own practice

  • Sara Blakely describes doing it: “What have you failed at this week?” (CNBC, 16 Oct 2013; as reported.)

Keep a journal

2 people: 1 recommend it, 1 describe their own practice only. Theme: Mind.

Recommend it to others

  • Tim Ferriss (author and podcaster, not a company founder) tells others to: “You should check it out.” (The Tim Ferriss Show transcript (PDF), 18 Sep 2015; own words. A weak recommendation that names a product, the Five-Minute Journal, so there may be a promotional tie.)

Describe their own practice

  • Jack Dorsey describes doing it: “I try to do that every single day” (CNBC, 8 Apr 2019; as reported.)

What research we opened

  • Frattaroli, Psychological Bulletin, 2006 (146 randomised studies of expressive writing). Writing about personal, emotional topics had a small but positive average effect (r = .075). That is a different thing from a business log or a five-minute gratitude journal, so it supports these founders’ habits only loosely (our reading).

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment. Two lines at day end.

  • Jeff Bezos describes doing it: “you get paid to make a small number of high-quality decisions” (Economic Club of Washington transcript, 13 Sep 2018; own words.)

Skip meals / fast (extreme practice)

2 people: 0 recommend it, 2 describe their own practice only. Theme: Body.

Describe their own practice

  • Jack Dorsey describes doing it: “Dorsey only eats dinner.” (CNBC, 8 Apr 2019; as reported.)
  • Nikhil Kamath describes doing it: “I do intermittent fasting such that my first meal is at 2 pm.” (Indian Express, 28 Mar 2025; as reported.)

What research we opened

  • Allaf and colleagues, Cochrane review, 2021 (intermittent fasting and heart-disease risk). In the short term, weight was lower with intermittent fasting than with eating freely (about 2.9 kg, 7 studies, low certainty). Against ordinary calorie cutting the result was uncertain. The authors judged the differences not clinically significant, noted some mild headaches, and called for studies in people with diabetes or eating disorders.

Not medical advice. Please see a doctor before you change how you eat. We are not suggesting you try this one. Some of these routines are extreme and the evidence is mixed.

Recommend it to others

  • Warren Buffett (investor, not a founder) tells others to: “it changed my life” (CNBC, 5 Dec 2018; as reported. We did not open the video; he is an investor.)

Evidence not checked We opened no study on this habit. The founders’ own words above are all we have.

The closest research we opened is general, not about this habit: a study of deliberate practice (Macnamara, Hambrick and Oswald, Psychological Science, 2014; PubMed record). A correction was published in 2018, and we did not read the changed figures, so we quote none of its numbers. It is not about public-speaking courses.

Our suggestion Try it as a small experiment.

How long does a new habit take?

A 2009 study by Lally and colleagues (96 volunteers, 12 weeks, one daily behaviour such as eating or drinking something) found that the time to reach the person’s own plateau of automatic behaviour ranged from 18 to 254 days, and that missing one day did not materially affect it (abstract). The behaviours were simple and the reports were self-reported. There is no single number of days. We do not quote the “66 days” figure because we did not find it on the page we opened.

What we did not check

We opened no research on: reading, early rising, meetings focus, fewer decisions, priority lists, networking, cold exposure, time-blocking, chronotype / early rising and success. We also opened a study of gratitude exercises but left it out, because no founder in our sample describes a gratitude practice. People we looked for and could not source are not shown, so a missing name is not a judgement.
