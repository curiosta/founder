India by place: stereotypes, quirks and what the data says

What people say about places in India, who says it, what the numbers show, and where they do not match. Built from the research record, with the sources and the doubts shown.

Read this first. These are attributed generalisations, not facts about people. Each shows who holds it, what the data says, and how sure we are. Caste, religion and slurs are excluded. Data shows averages for a place, not any individual.

Stereotype, quirk or belief: what is the difference?

Stereotype

A simple claim about what the people of a place are like or do, such as "this city is unsafe" or "that state loves sweets". It is repeated by many people, often without checking. Some turn out partly true on the numbers. Some do not.

Quirk or belief

A custom, local story or belief tied to a place, such as a haunted fort or a long afternoon chat. We label these as beliefs. We do not say they are true. We say what the record shows and, where there is data, what it can and cannot test.

Each row is an attributed generalisation: we say who holds the view (a newspaper, a survey, a government, locals), what the data says, the best counter-view, and how sure we are. Data describes the average for a place. It says nothing about any one person who lives there.

This is a sourced sample, not a complete map of India. 34 of the 36 states and union territories have at least one row shown. A place with no row says "not yet sourced". That means we have not found and read a source yet, not that no stereotype exists. Rows are added when a source is opened and checked.

                                          Sources and method

                                          The source standard

                                          Every row names where the claim comes from. Each source is typed (government primary, academic, news or reference, industry or advocacy body, practitioner or reference lead), so you can see what kind of source it is. Industry bodies and tourism pages describe themselves, so they are marked as such. A row needs at least two independent sources that we opened to rise above "low" confidence. Anything we only saw as a search excerpt, an abstract or a lead says so. No claim on this page rests on text written by an AI tool.

                                          What the labels mean

                                          • Verdict. "Data supports it", "contradicts it", "is mixed" or "cannot test it". Many claims, such as "people are courteous", cannot be tested with data at all.
                                          • Confidence. Confidence in the evidence check and in the attribution of who holds it. 'low' = fewer than two independent sources opened or numbers unverified.
                                          • Read in full, read in part, search excerpt only, abstract only, not opened. How much of the source we saw, and on what date.
                                          • Link check. On 26 Oct 2026 we tried each link from our own server (url_check in the dataset). "Link opened (HTTP 200)" means the page answered. "Could not open" means our check was refused or timed out; many publishers block automated visitors, so the link may work in a browser. It does not mean the page is gone or wrong.
                                          • Provisional, OCR-derived, indicative only. NFHS-6 numbers are provisional. Some state pages of the NFHS-6 compendium are images; those figures were read by character recognition and are marked. UPI state figures follow the bank or app, not where people live.
